# Society Governance Policy

## General Meetings

The Society holds one Annual General Meeting (AGM) each calendar year, as
required by the Societies Act. At the AGM the directors are elected, the
audited financial statements are presented and accepted by the membership,
and the auditor for the coming year is appointed.

A Special General Meeting (SGM) is any general meeting other than the AGM.
An SGM may be called by the board at its own discretion, or must be called
by the board on the written request of at least 10 percent of the voting
membership. An SGM is the correct instrument where a decision cannot
properly wait until the next AGM — for example a material change to the
mandate, an extraordinary financial matter, or a change to the bylaws.

Notice of any general meeting must be sent to all members at their
registered email address not fewer than 14 days and not more than 60 days
before the meeting. The notice must include the agenda and the text of any
special resolution to be considered. Business not identified in the notice
may not be voted on at the meeting.

## Quorum and Voting

Quorum for a general meeting is 15 voting members present, or 10 percent of
the voting membership, whichever is greater. If quorum is not reached within
30 minutes of the appointed start time, the meeting is adjourned to the same
day the following week, at which point those present constitute quorum.

Ordinary resolutions pass on a simple majority of votes cast. Special
resolutions — including any change to the bylaws, the mandate, or the
disposal of substantially all of the Society's assets — require two thirds
of votes cast. Proxy voting is not permitted.

## Financial Reporting to Members

The board must present the Society's financial position to the membership at
each general meeting, including current reserves, the operating position
against budget for the year to date, and any material change in expected
funding.

Where the Society's unrestricted reserves fall below three months of
operating expenditure, the board is required to inform the membership at the
earliest general meeting and to present a written plan for restoring
reserves. The board may not defer this disclosure to the next scheduled AGM.

## Volunteers

Volunteers are central to the Society's work and are not a substitute for
adequately funded staff positions. Every volunteer receives a written role
description, an orientation before beginning work, and a named staff member
as their point of contact.

Volunteer orientation covers the Society's mission and mandate, the specific
goals of the role, boundaries of the role, and the process for raising a
concern. Where a volunteer's conduct falls short of the role description,
the responsible staff member addresses it directly and promptly; unaddressed
conduct issues are consistently the largest source of volunteer attrition.

The Society records volunteer hours annually and reports the total to the
membership at the AGM.

## Membership

Membership is open to any person who supports the objects of the Society and
pays the annual membership fee. Voting rights begin 30 days after membership
is granted, so that a meeting cannot be decided by members recruited
immediately before it.

Membership lapses if the annual fee is unpaid for 90 days. A lapsed member
may be reinstated on payment without a new 30-day waiting period, provided
reinstatement occurs within one year.
